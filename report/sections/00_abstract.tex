\system is a retrieval-first pipeline for low-latency video corpus moment retrieval: it narrows the corpus with cheap subtitle-based retrieval before applying expensive multimodal localization. This report studies that accuracy-runtime trade-off on the \dataset validation split under a fixed-feature evaluation setup. We evaluate \bm, dense sentence-transformer retrieval, and sparse--dense fusion as inexpensive subtitle-search baselines, then use dense retrieval as a routing stage before \xml, the cross-modal localizer used for the final VCMR result. The selected dense top-5 $\rightarrow$ \xml pipeline improves VR R@1 from 1.69 to 11.56 and VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 from 0.38 to 1.43, while keeping SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 unchanged at 15.60. At the same time, average validation wall-clock time drops from 7.36 to 4.96\,ms/query, giving a 1.48$\times$ speedup over full \xml. The main takeaway is that cheap subtitle-based retrieval is most useful as a systems-level router: it decides where the expensive cross-modal model should spend computation, rather than replacing multimodal grounding.
