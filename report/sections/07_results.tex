\subsection{Accuracy-Latency Trade-off}
Among the transcript-only front-end options, weighted fusion with $\alpha = 0.25$ is the best validation retriever, but its gains over dense-only retrieval are small. This result matters mainly because it confirms that the main systems leverage is not in squeezing out another fraction of a point from subtitle-window ranking. The more important question is what happens once the chosen retriever controls how much work \xml must do downstream.

The full multimodal \xml baseline exposes the bottleneck clearly. Its within-video localization quality is strong, with SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 equal to 15.60, but its corpus-level ranking is weak in this validation run: VR R@1 is 1.69 and VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 is 0.38. It also takes 7.36\,ms/query in the fixed-feature validation setup. This is exactly the regime where a staged pipeline should help: use a cheap retriever to find plausible videos, then let \xml handle fine-grained localization.

Our main reported operating point is dense top-5 shortlist $\rightarrow$ \xml. That pipeline raises VR R@1 from 1.69 to 11.56 and VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 from 0.38 to 1.43, while keeping SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 unchanged at 15.60. Average validation wall-clock time drops from 7.36\,ms/query for full \xml to 4.96\,ms/query, a 1.48$\times$ speedup. The runtime decomposition in \cref{tab:latency-breakdown} shows why this works: only about 0.39\,ms/query is spent on the dense front-end, while the dominant savings come from reducing the amount of expensive multimodal inference. Dense retrieval contributes the video-level recall, and \xml remains the component that delivers the final localization quality.

\subsection{Ablations}
We focus on the two ablations that directly test the \xml-stage design. The first is shortlist depth. Top-1 is the fastest shortlisted setting at 4.88\,ms/query and gives the best VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5, namely 1.57, for a 1.51$\times$ speedup over full \xml. However, its candidate set is brittle: only 11.56\% of queries retain the gold video in the one-video shortlist. Moving to top-5 almost doubles shortlist video recall to 22.29\% with only a 0.08\,ms/query increase in validation wall-clock time, while keeping VR R@1 at 11.56 and preserving SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5. Top-10 pushes shortlist video recall further to 28.44\%, but its VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 does not improve over top-5 and the runtime edge shrinks to 1.46$\times$. For that reason, top-5 is the cleaner balanced operating point.

The second ablation is shared compaction as an alternative way to cut \xml cost. It is informative mainly as negative evidence. Plain shared compaction with keep ratio 0.6 drops SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 to 11.23 and is slower than full \xml at 11.11\,ms/query, or only 0.66$\times$ as fast. Adding the dense front-end before shared compaction recovers some corpus-level quality, but it still underperforms dense shortlist $\rightarrow$ \xml, reaching VCMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 of 1.17 and SVMR R@1 at IoU 0.5 of 11.56 at 10.46\,ms/query. The compaction plumbing shows that reducing sequence length is structurally feasible, but the current cheap visual-change selector discards too much relevant context. That makes shared compaction a useful negative ablation rather than the main system result.
